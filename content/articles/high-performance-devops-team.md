What does it mean to have a strong software team? From improved DevOps performance to faster software delivery, dev teams have many different needs and goals. Let’s look into how you can make your business stand out.

We’ll explore how combining DORA metrics with core business strategies, Agile methodologies, and cutting-edge automation in software development can empower organizations to deliver not only efficient software but also tangible business outcomes.

## DORA Metrics: How to Evaluate DevOps Performance

First things first; how do you establish a baseline for a successful, high-performing software team? For many, DORA metrics have become the de facto standard for calculating software development and delivery success.

DORA metrics set themselves with [four standard metrics keys](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics-four-keys/) that measure the performance, maturity and success of [DevOps](https://www.oliant.io/articles/what-is-devops-best-practices) processes. These are:

-   **Deployment frequency**: The average number of code deployments to production each day
-   **Lead time for changes**: The amount of time it takes for a code change to be deployed to production
-   **Change failure rate**: The number of failed changes divided by the total number of changes made over a period of time.
-   **Time to restore service**: How quickly an organization can recover from failures or incidents in production

These can provide valuable insights, but it’s also important to keep in mind what’s actually meaningful to your business and products.

Ori Keren, co-founder and CEO of LinearB, has commented on their [Software Engenieering Benchmark Report](https://linearb.io/resources/engineering-benchmarks) that these metrics help teams move “from darkness to visibility” by highlighting efficiency in development practices. Yet, Keren warns that while these metrics act as a finely tuned engine, they do not steer the car. In other words, efficient processes alone do not guarantee that the team is headed in the right direction.

If an organization wishes to reach elite status, the report suggests they should embed business goals in the team’s workflow, and anchor check-ins and recurring ceremonies around those goals.

Keren also highlighted the importance of automation in these areas. “With things like generative AI, code is being written fast. But the process of orchestrating everything that needs to happen to the code from when it’s written until reaching production, all those phases are definitely where more automation can help.”

By bringing [Agile Methodologies](https://www.oliant.io/articles/agile-vs-scrum) and DevOps practices, as well as value stream management and business agility, into strategic planning, teams can modernize their development processes and become elite engineering organizations.

## What Makes a High-Performing Software Team?

Why do some software teams thrive while others struggle? Mark Cruth, modern work coach for Atlassian, highlighted four key elements:

-   **Clarity of purpose:** Teams should deeply understand why they’re building something, not just what they’re building. The goal isn’t just to develop software; it’s to solve a real problem.
-   **Decentralized decision-making:** The best decisions come from those doing the work, not from leadership two levels up. Giving teams ownership over decisions leads to better outcomes.
-   **High trust environment:** Teams that want to truly gel should feel both trust and psychological safety within their work environment. This eliminates friction and stress that can cause bumps in the development process.
-   **Embracing uncertainty:** In software, certainty is a myth. The best teams accept uncertainty and use it as a tool for innovation and adaptability.

Speaking at [ScanAgile 2023](https://www.scan-agile.org/), Cruth emphasized that high performance isn’t about adopting a rigid framework or hiring expert coaches—it’s about *intentional experimentation*. Instead of overhauling everything at once, start small: test a new practice, see if it helps, and build from there.

Cruth suggests a few practical ways to improve team dynamics, including creating team vision statements, regular retrospectives focused on team collaboration, and improv games to boost creativity and adaptability.

However, many strategies can create an elite development team, so let’s look into some of them.

## Core Strategies for Building High-Performing Software Teams

To drive both technical and business success, adopting a multifaceted approach is a must. Here are core strategies that integrate essential technical metrics with practices that enhance overall team performance:

### Purpose-Driven Development

A clear sense of purpose is the cornerstone of every successful project. High-performing teams begin by asking critical questions such as, “Do you know why you’re building that feature?” and “What problem are we trying to solve?”.

This clarity ensures that every decision, from selecting Agile methodologies to implementing automation in software development, is rooted in solving real business challenges. When the team understands the ‘why’ behind their tasks, they are more likely to contribute meaningfully to business outcomes.

### Embracing Agile Methodologies

Implementing Agile methodologies like Scrum or Kanban enables teams to adapt quickly to changing requirements. Agile practices foster incremental development and frequent feedback, allowing teams to refine their processes continuously.

By breaking work into smaller, manageable chunks, teams can rapidly iterate on features and adjust priorities in line with evolving business needs. This approach not only improves DevOps performance but also ensures that every sprint contributes to the strategic goals of the organization.

### Fostering Team Collaboration and Psychological Safety

Team collaboration is essential for innovation and problem-solving. A collaborative environment where every team member feels valued is built on a foundation of psychological safety. This means creating an atmosphere where individuals are comfortable sharing ideas, taking risks, and learning from failures without fear of retribution.

When teams combine open communication with trust, they are better positioned to tackle complex challenges and drive meaningful improvements in DevOps performance.

**Leveraging Automation in Software Development**

The integration of automation in software development is a critical factor in reducing friction throughout the delivery process. Modern tools, including those leveraging generative AI, can automate routine tasks—from code reviews and testing to deployment and monitoring.

Automation speeds up software delivery and minimizes human error, allowing teams to focus on strategic problem-solving. By streamlining processes, teams can better meet the demands of rapid iteration while ensuring high-quality outcomes.

### Embedding Business Goals into Daily Workflows

For technical teams to succeed, every action must be linked to the organization’s strategic objectives. Embedding business outcomes into daily workflows means aligning sprint goals, performance reviews, and team ceremonies with broader business targets.

This integration ensures that the pursuit of technical excellence, as measured by DORA metrics, does not occur in isolation but contributes directly to the company’s success.

### Prioritizing Continuous Learning and Adaptability

The technology landscape is constantly evolving, and teams must remain agile to keep pace. Continuous learning—through training, conferences, and certifications—ensures that team members are equipped with the latest skills and knowledge.

This commitment to growth not only enhances DevOps performance, it also prepares the team to adapt to new challenges and opportunities. A learning culture empowers teams to experiment with innovative solutions, driving improvements in both technical and business outcomes.

### Recognizing and Rewarding Team Efforts

Recognition plays a significant role in maintaining team morale and motivation. Acknowledging individual and collective achievements fosters a sense of pride and encourages further innovation.

Whether it’s through monetary rewards, promotions, or public recognition, celebrating successes reinforces a positive feedback loop. When team members feel appreciated, team collaboration flourishes, ultimately boosting software delivery and DevOps performance.

### Addressing Conflicts Proactively

In any collaborative environment, conflicts are inevitable. Addressing these issues promptly and constructively is vital for maintaining psychological safety. Leaders must foster an environment where concerns can be openly discussed and resolved.

Proactive conflict management prevents small issues from escalating and helps maintain a cohesive team dynamic. By ensuring that disagreements are seen as opportunities for improvement, teams can enhance both their software delivery and overall performance.

### Measuring Performance Holistically

As we mentioned, while DORA metrics provide a quantitative assessment of team performance, they should be complemented by qualitative insights. Combining hard data with feedback from team members and stakeholders offers a holistic view of how well the team is functioning.

Regular performance reviews that assess both technical achievements and contributions to business outcomes ensure that teams remain aligned with organizational goals. This comprehensive approach to measurement is key to sustained success.

## Conclusion

High-performing software teams come in many forms, but they excel when they integrate technical efficiency with strategic, business-focused practices, embracing Agile methodologies, fostering team collaboration and psychological safety, and promoting decentralized decision-making.

Moreover, the strategic use of automation in software development and a commitment to continuous learning enable teams to adapt rapidly in a dynamic environment. As the digital landscape evolves, the ability to balance technical precision with strategic vision will determine which teams stand out.
